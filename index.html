THE RECONSTRUCTION

An experiment in earth.

De Worf is a medieval stinswier in Lies on Terschelling. The original research notebook explores whether soil excavated from its surrounding ditch could account for the mound.

The original geometry, in your browser

The real contour_mound polygon comes from de_worf.gpkg. By default the ditch extends 13 m outward with a 1.7 m depth limit; the mound rises over 8 m inward before reaching a plateau. Each dimension starts locked. Use its lock button to edit it; locking it again preserves your current value. Reset restores all notebook defaults and locks all three dimensions.

Ditch depthmin(distance to edge × tan(α), depth limit)Mound heightmin(inward distance, slope width) × tan(β)

Changing ditch width recomputes the outward buffer and the 0.5 m raster distance transform in your browser. Changing the depth limit updates the depth cap; changing inward slope width updates the plateau and mound volume. Mound volume integrates inward-buffer areas at 0.05 m intervals. These are the quantities shown in the live results.

Two views, two height references

Reconstruction puts the hypothetical mound and ditch on a common zero surface. Heights are relative, not NAP. This view combines the notebook’s relative models; it is not a historical terrain measurement.

AHN5 terrain shows the actual 0.5 m DTM in metres above NAP. Changing angles does not alter the measured terrain. The live metrics continue to describe your reconstruction.

The display mound is rasterised on the AHN grid, with a slightly different alignment from the notebook’s separate mound raster. Reported mound volumes retain the notebook’s vector integration. Missing AHN cells remain gaps.

How to interpret a scenario

Angles are measured from horizontal. A gentle ditch may not reach its selected depth limit; steeper sides produce a flat bottom. Ditch width can vary from 1 to 25 m (inside the prepared terrain crop), depth limit from 0.1 to 5 m, and inward slope width from 0.5 to 14 m (retaining a plateau inside this contour). The soil budget assumes one cubic metre excavated equals one cubic metre deposited, without compaction, erosion or imported soil. A deficit or surplus is a comparison, not an archaeological conclusion.
